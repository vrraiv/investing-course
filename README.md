# Macro Economist → Institutional Macro Investor

A 24-week, project-based curriculum designed for an experienced professional macroeconomist moving toward investment roles in global macro, rates/FX, tactical asset allocation, and total-portfolio management.

## Design principles

This is not an economics course. It assumes strong macroeconomic knowledge and focuses on the missing bridge:

**view → market pricing → instrument → position → portfolio → risk → P&L → attribution**

The curriculum is deliberately interactive. Each module contains:
- learning objectives;
- core concepts;
- practical exercises;
- a market or portfolio lab;
- an investment-committee deliverable;
- self-test questions;
- a rubric for completion.

## Target outcome

By the end, you should be able to:
1. Read rates, FX, inflation, and cross-asset markets as an investor rather than only as an economist.
2. Translate a macro forecast into a relative-to-market thesis.
3. Choose among cash bonds, futures, swaps/OIS, FX spot/forwards, and options conceptually.
4. Calculate and reason with DV01, duration, convexity, carry, roll, leverage, volatility, correlations, and risk budgets.
5. Construct outright, curve, relative-value, and cross-market trades.
6. Size positions and combine them into a portfolio.
7. Conduct scenario analysis and distinguish forecast risk from portfolio risk.
8. Maintain an auditable investment journal and simulated track record.
9. Attribute P&L and distinguish good process from good luck.
10. Present an investment case in the style expected of an institutional investment team.

## Suggested cadence

**24 weeks, ~5–7 hours/week**

- 2 hours: concepts/readings
- 1–2 hours: coding/data lab
- 1 hour: current-market exercise
- 1 hour: portfolio journal / trade review
- 30–60 min: self-test or investment memo

Do not wait until you "know enough" to begin the portfolio. Start the shadow portfolio in Week 2.

## Course map

| Phase | Weeks | Focus |
|---|---:|---|
| I | 1–4 | Investor mindset and fixed-income foundations |
| II | 5–8 | Rates instruments, curves, carry and relative value |
| III | 9–12 | FX, inflation, cross-asset macro and derivatives |
| IV | 13–16 | Portfolio construction, risk and sizing |
| V | 17–20 | Macro investment process and systematic tools |
| VI | 21–24 | Institutional PM practice and capstone |

## How to use the course

Treat each week as a gate, not merely reading. A module is complete only when you can **apply** the concepts to a current or historical market situation without relying on definitions.

Maintain four running artifacts throughout:
1. **Market dashboard**
2. **Investment journal**
3. **Shadow portfolio**
4. **Investment casebook**

Those four artifacts become evidence of investment capability.
